Emission and spectrum

The evaporation cross section, the sequence-by-sequence solution it drives, and the prompt neutron spectrum that follows from it. The sequential solution and the sequence average are the model of Tudora, Hambsch and Tobosaru [1], with the systematics of its residual quantities [2]; the evaporation spectrum in closed form is Madland and Nix [8], its laboratory transformation and the probability of emission along a chain follow Terrell [9], Märten and Seeliger [11] and Kawano et al. [12], and the weights of the total spectrum are the conventions of the evaluated spectra [10].

DeterministicSequentialEmission.MINIMUM_TEMPERATURE — Constant
MINIMUM_TEMPERATURE

Lower bound on an acceptable residual temperature, in MeV; the convention of the thesis.

A numerical floor, not the crossover from the Fermi-gas to the constant-temperature regime, which lies near T ≈ 0.5–0.8 MeV for fission fragments. It rejects roots only where Ē_r − Sₙ has fallen below about 17 keV, truncating a chain that is already ending. Applied unconditionally, unlike the optional upper bound.

DeterministicSequentialEmission.ConstantCrossSection — Type
ConstantCrossSection()

Energy-independent σ_c(ε) = σ₀.

The evaporation spectrum and its first moment become

φ(ε; T) = (ε/T²) exp(−ε/T)
⟨ε⟩(T)  = 2T

so the sequential equation a T² + 2T − (Ē_r − Sₙ) = 0 is quadratic with the positive root

T = [√(1 + a(Ē_r − Sₙ)) − 1] / a

DeterministicSequentialEmission.EvaporationCrossSection — Type
EvaporationCrossSection

Analytical form of the cross section for compound nucleus formation in the process inverse to evaporation, σ_c(ε).

The choice determines how the sequential equations are solved: a constant cross section reduces them to a quadratic with a closed-form root, while an energy-dependent one makes them transcendental.

source
DeterministicSequentialEmission.VariableCrossSection — Type
VariableCrossSection()

Iwamoto's energy-dependent form, a constant term plus one carrying the s-wave neutron strength function,

σ_c(ε) = σ₀ (1 + α/√ε),   α = π ħ² S₀ / (m r₀² A^(2/3))

which gives

φ(ε; T) = (ε + α√ε) exp(−ε/T) / [T^(3/2)(√T + α√π/2)]
⟨ε⟩(T)  = T (2√T + 3α√π/4) / (√T + α√π/2)

The sequential equation is then transcendental and is solved numerically. It has exactly one positive root: f(0) = −(Ē_r − Sₙ) < 0, f → +∞, and f′ > 0 throughout, with f′(0) = 3/2.

DeterministicSequentialEmission.neutron_strength_function — Method
neutron_strength_function(A) -> Float64

The s-wave neutron strength function S₀(A), a piecewise-linear fit, with plateaux where the data support one, of the RIPL-3 resonance data, Capote et al., Nucl. Data Sheets 110, 3107 (2009), doi:10.1016/j.nds.2009.10.004. Returned bare, not in the customary units of 10⁻⁴: S₀(140) is 2.33e-4, as alpha_parameter expects. As a function of A alone it cannot represent the Z-dependence of S₀, largest at A = 111–124 and across the N = 82 closure at A = 136–146. The resulting α, from 0.037 at A = 120 to 1.24 at A = 147, extends beyond the band 0.21–0.55 over which the variable cross section was validated against an optical-model calculation.

DeterministicSequentialEmission.EmissionChain — Type
EmissionChain

The full sequential emission from one initial fragment at one (A, Z, TKE), characterised by two distinct counts.

Fields

  • possible: n, the number of prompt neutrons emitted. The chain continues while the residual can afford a separation energy once the kinetic energy carried away by the neutrons already emitted is returned. n is an integer, so the post-neutron fragment mass A_p = A − n is an integer without rounding.
  • solved: k_max ≤ n, the number of sequences whose equation admits an acceptable temperature. T, Ē_r and ⟨ε⟩ are averaged over these k_max sequences only.

DeterministicSequentialEmission.SequenceWeighting — Type
SequenceWeighting

How the emission sequences of one fragment are weighted in a quantity carried by its neutrons: the centre-of-mass and laboratory spectra and the mean neutron energy.

neutron_multiplicity is ν = (n+1)/2, the mean of a number of emitted neutrons that is equiprobable on 1 … n. The k-th neutron of the chain is then emitted with probability P(N ≥ k) = (n+1−k)/n, and a quantity averaged over the emitted neutrons carries that weight:

⟨q⟩ = Σ_k (n+1−k) q_k / Σ_k (n+1−k)

EmissionProbabilityWeighting, the default, applies it. The weights fall linearly along the chain, as the probability of reaching a step does in every treatment that carries a distribution of excitation energy whose width is comparable with one neutron step: Terrell, Phys. Rev. 113, 527 (1959), doi:10.1103/PhysRev.113.527; Märten and Seeliger, J. Phys. G 10, 349 (1984), doi:10.1088/0305-4616/10/3/010; Kawano et al., Phys. Rev. C 104, 014611 (2021), doi:10.1103/PhysRevC.104.014611. UniformSequenceWeighting counts every sequence once, ⟨q⟩ = (1/k_max) Σ_k q_k, the operator of eq. (12) of Tudora, EPJA 54, 87 (2018), doi:10.1140/epja/i2018-12521-7, and of the thesis. It takes the last neutron of a chain as emitted as often as the first, which over-represents the cold end of the chain and gives a spectrum softer than the multiplicity it goes with implies.

The weighting applies to what the neutrons carry. The residual temperature and the residual energy written against fragment mass belong to the nucleus the chain ends on; with the count equiprobable on 1 … n each residual is the last with the same probability, so their average over the sequences is uniform under either weighting.

DeterministicSequentialEmission.emission_chain — Method
emission_chain(table, model, cross_section, fragment, excitation; kwargs...)
    -> EmissionChain

Solve the sequential equations for one fragment, starting from its excitation energy at full acceleration. Two stages separate k_max from n.

The first solves

Ē_r^(k-1) − Sₙ^(k-1) − ⟨ε⟩_k = a_k T_k²

while the residual can afford the separation energy, consuming Sₙ + ⟨ε⟩ per step. It stops at the first sequence whose equation admits no acceptable temperature.

The second returns the kinetic energy carried away by the neutrons emitted so far and continues on Sₙ alone, counting the emissions that remain energetically possible. Those sequences carry no temperature but do count towards n.

DeterministicSequentialEmission.neutron_multiplicity — Method
neutron_multiplicity(chain) -> Float64

The prompt neutron multiplicity ν, zero for a chain that emits nothing.

ν is the sequence average of the emission index. Every per-sequence quantity q_k is reduced by the same operator, eq. (12) of Tudora, EPJA 54, 87 (2018), doi:10.1140/epja/i2018-12521-7; eq. (4) of Tudora, EPJA 56, 84 (2020), doi:10.1140/epja/s10050-020-00059-2; eq. (20) of Tudora, EPJ Web Conf. 256, 00017 (2021), doi:10.1051/epjconf/202125600017,

⟨q⟩ = (1/n) Σ_{k=1}^{n} q_k

evaluated at q_k = k, so ν = (1 + 2 + … + n)/n = (n+1)/2. It is the multiplicity of the averaged emission sequence and the quantity compared with measurement. It differs from n, the number of sequences, which post_neutron_mass uses.

DeterministicSequentialEmission.post_neutron_mass — Method
post_neutron_mass(chain) -> Int

The mass number of the last residual fragment, A_p = A − n, an integer by construction.

A_p uses n, not ν: it is a property of the chain, the nucleus on which the cascade ends, rather than a sequence average. A global treatment yields only an averaged ν(A,Z,TKE), whose A − ν must be rounded (Tudora, EPJA 58, 126 (2022), doi:10.1140/epja/s10050-022-00766-y, §2.4). Consequently ⟨A⟩ − ⟨A_p⟩ equals the yield average of n, not ⟨ν⟩.

DeterministicSequentialEmission.SpectrumAverage — Type
SpectrumAverage

How the pair spectra of the fragmentations combine into the total laboratory spectrum.

PerNeutronAverage, the default, weights each unit-normalised pair spectrum by the yield times the pair multiplicity, N = Σ Y ν_pair N_pair / Σ Y ν_pair. Every emitted neutron counts once, so this is the unit-area shape χ/ν̄ of the neutron yield per fission that evaluated spectra represent: eq. (99) of Capote et al., Nucl. Data Sheets 131, 1 (2016), doi:10.1016/j.nds.2015.12.002. It is also the weight of eq. (53) of Madland and Nix, Nucl. Sci. Eng. 81, 213 (1982), doi:10.13182/NSE82-5, across fission chances, and of eqs. (8)–(9) of Kawano et al., Phys. Rev. C 104, 014611 (2021), doi:10.1103/PhysRevC.104.014611, across fragment pairs. PerFissionAverage weights it by the yield alone, N = Σ Y N_pair / Σ Y, the form of the thesis and of Tudora, Hambsch, Tobosaru, EPJA 54, 87 (2018), doi:10.1140/epja/i2018-12521-7, text after eq. (16). The two differ where ν_pair correlates with the hardness of N_pair, and the per-fission form under-weights the hot, high-multiplicity fragmentations.

DeterministicSequentialEmission.SpectrumWeighting — Type
SpectrumWeighting

How the light and heavy fragment spectra are combined into the pair spectrum.

MultiplicityWeighting uses ν_{L,H}/ν_pair, eq. (16) of Tudora, EPJA 54, 87 (2018), doi:10.1140/epja/i2018-12521-7, and eq. (33) of Capote et al., Nucl. Data Sheets 131, 1 (2016), doi:10.1016/j.nds.2015.12.002; it is the convention of the thesis and the one the pipeline applies. ExcitationWeighting uses E*_{L,H}/TXE, eq. (31) of Tudora, EPJ Web Conf. 256, 00017 (2021), doi:10.1051/epjconf/202125600017. The two are close by construction, since E*_H/TXE behaves like ν_H/(ν_L + ν_H), but not identical. Only MultiplicityWeighting combined with PerNeutronAverage makes the weight of a fragment spectrum in the total its neutron count, Y ν_{L,H}; ExcitationWeighting with the same average is a hybrid.

DeterministicSequentialEmission.centre_of_mass_spectrum — Method
centre_of_mass_spectrum(cross_section, α, T, ε) -> Float64

The Weisskopf-Ewing evaporation spectrum of one emission sequence, in the centre-of-mass frame of the emitting fragment, normalized to unit area.

φ(ε; T) = K(T) ε σ_c(ε) exp(−ε/T),   K(T) = [∫₀^∞ ε σ_c(ε) exp(−ε/T) dε]⁻¹

σ₀ cancels in K, so only the shape of σ_c survives. With a constant cross section this is ε exp(−ε/T)/T²; with Iwamoto's σ₀(1 + α/√ε) it is (ε + α√ε) exp(−ε/T) / [T^(3/2)(√T + α√π/2)]. Both integrate to 1, and their first moments are 2T and mean_neutron_energy respectively.

α is ignored by the constant cross section and is the residual nucleus's alpha_parameter for the variable one, the value the sequential equation was solved with, so the spectrum is consistent with the energy balance that produced T.

source
DeterministicSequentialEmission.chain_centre_of_mass_spectrum — Method
chain_centre_of_mass_spectrum(chain, cross_section, ε[, weighting]) -> Float64

The sequence-averaged centre-of-mass spectrum of one fragment, Φ(ε) = Σ_{k solved} w_k φ_k(ε) / Σ_{k solved} w_k, with w_k the sequence_weight of weighting, uniform (w_k = 1, denominator k_max) where none is given.

Same weights and the same skip rule as chain_spectrum. This is the quantity measured centre-of-mass spectra are compared against, and its first moment is sequence_average(chain, :mean_neutron_energy, weighting) by construction.

DeterministicSequentialEmission.chain_spectrum — Method
chain_spectrum(chain, cross_section, E_f, E[, weighting]) -> Float64

The laboratory spectrum of one fragment, averaged over the emission sequences that carry a temperature.

N_f(E) = Σ_{k solved} w_k N(E; α_k, T_k, E_f) / Σ_{k solved} w_k

w_k is the sequence_weight of weighting, uniform (w_k = 1, denominator k_max) where none is given. The uniform case is the average of sequence_average and of eq. (12) of Tudora, EPJA 54, 87 (2018), doi:10.1140/epja/i2018-12521-7. Sequences without a temperature carry no spectrum and are skipped, although they count towards n and hence towards the multiplicity weighting of the pair spectrum.

Returns 0 for a chain that solved nothing.

DeterministicSequentialEmission.laboratory_spectrum — Method
laboratory_spectrum(cross_section, α, T, E_f, E) -> Float64

One emission sequence's spectrum transformed into the laboratory frame.

A neutron leaving a fragment that moves with kinetic energy per nucleon E_f arrives with E = ε + E_f + 2√(ε E_f) cos θ. Isotropy in the centre-of-mass makes cos θ uniform, so at fixed ε the lab energy is uniform over a band of width 4√(ε E_f), and folding over φ:

N(E) = 1/(4√E_f) ∫_{u₁}^{u₂} φ(ε) ε^(-1/2) dε,   u_{1,2} = (√E ∓ √E_f)²

The factor 4√(ε E_f) is the width of that band, by the identity (√ε+√E_f)² − (√ε−√E_f)² = 4√(ε E_f), so the kernel is measure-preserving and N inherits unit area from φ exactly. With ε = s² the kernel is uniform in s over an interval of fixed length 2√E_f,

N(E) = 1/(2√E_f) ∫_{|√E−√E_f|}^{√E+√E_f} φ(s²) ds

Evaluated in closed form rather than by quadrature. With a_i = u_i/T,

constant σ_c:   N = [Γ(3/2,a₁) − Γ(3/2,a₂)] / (4√(E_f T))
variable σ_c:   N = { [Γ(3/2,a₁) − Γ(3/2,a₂)] + (α/√T)[exp(−a₁) − exp(−a₂)] }
                    / [4√E_f (√T + α√π/2)]

The second reduces to the first as α → 0. The differences are taken over the upper incomplete gamma, since the equivalent lower-gamma difference cancels catastrophically in the tail and is in error by a factor of four beyond about 20 MeV.

Source: eq. (12) of Madland & Nix, Nucl. Sci. Eng. 81, 213 (1982), doi:10.13182/NSE82-5.
