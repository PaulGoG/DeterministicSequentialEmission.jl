The excitation-energy partition

How the total excitation energy of a fragmentation is divided between its fragments: by a tabulated E*_H/TXE (A_H), by a temperature ratio R_T(A_H) through the relation E*_H/TXE = 1/(1 + R_a R_T²), or by modelling at scission. The curves, the level density parameters and the relation come from the domain package; see The fragmentation domain. The partition by the temperature ratio and its relation to the excitation-energy fraction are those of the point-by-point model [6], as applied in the sequential treatment [1]; the ratio itself, extracted from measured multiplicities, is [7].

ExcitationEnergies

The excitation energies of the two fully accelerated fragments, in MeV. By construction light + heavy == TXE.

DeterministicSequentialEmission.ExcitationEnergyRatio — Type
ExcitationEnergyRatio(curve)

Partition by E*_H/TXE as a function of A_H, supplied as a segmented curve. The ratio is obtained from modelling at scission in a separate code and supplied as a tabulated file, without a run manifest; TemperatureRatio, by contrast, is resolved through a run record. Every point must lie in (0, 1).

DeterministicSequentialEmission.PartitionMethod — Type
PartitionMethod

How the total excitation energy is divided between the complementary fragments at full acceleration. ScissionModelling models the division at scission; the two curve-based methods divide TXE directly at full acceleration. The curves quote no uncertainty, so E*_L and E*_H carry none and uncertainty propagation stops at TXE.

source
DeterministicSequentialEmission.ScissionModelling — Type
ScissionModelling(deformation, model)

Partition by modelling at scission, using extra-deformation energies.

Two assumptions fix the split. The fragments are far more deformed at scission than at full acceleration, and the difference

ΔE_def = E_def(β₂^sc) − E_def(β₂^a.t.)

reappears as excitation energy of the fully accelerated fragment. And the complementary fragments are in thermal equilibrium at scission with Fermi-gas level densities, so T_L^sc = T_H^sc gives E_sc^L/E_sc^H = a_L/a_H. Together:

E_sc^{L,H} = [TXE − (ΔE_def^L + ΔE_def^H)] / (1 + a_{H,L}/a_{L,H})
E*_{L,H}   = E_sc^{L,H} + ΔE_def^{L,H}
source
DeterministicSequentialEmission.TemperatureRatio — Type
TemperatureRatio(curve, model)

Partition by the ratio of fragment temperatures at full acceleration, R_T = T_L/T_H, as a function of A_H.

With both fragments in the Fermi-gas regime, E*_{L,H} = a_{L,H} T_{L,H}² gives

E*_H/TXE = 1 / (1 + (a_L/a_H) R_T²)

so the temperature ratio and the level density parameters together fix the split. Every point of the curve must be positive.

DeterministicSequentialEmission.partition — Method
partition(method, fragmentation, TXE) -> Union{ExcitationEnergies,Nothing}

Divide TXE between the complementary fragments, or nothing when the method cannot be evaluated for this fragmentation — a mass outside the curve, a missing level density parameter, an absent deformation energy, or a division that leaves either fragment with negative excitation energy.

A split into two copies of the same nuclide divides TXE evenly, and does so before the partition method is consulted.

DeterministicSequentialEmission.segment_coverage — Method
segment_coverage(method, heavy_masses) -> NamedTuple

The heavy masses of a sweep covered by the partition input: covered, the fraction reached, and uncovered, the masses not reached. A curve carries its own fit range, independent of the swept domain. partition returns nothing outside that range, which is indistinguishable downstream from a physically excluded fragmentation.

Examples

julia> method = ExcitationEnergyRatio(SegmentedCurve([(126, 0.5), (150, 0.6)]));

julia> report = segment_coverage(method, 126:174);

julia> report.covered
0.5102040816326531

julia> first(report.uncovered), last(report.uncovered)
(151, 174)
