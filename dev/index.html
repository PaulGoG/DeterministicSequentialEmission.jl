DeterministicSequentialEmission

DeterministicSequentialEmission.jl calculates prompt neutron emission from fission fragments in low-energy and thermal fission, tracking de-excitation cascades sequence by sequence across the complete pre-neutron fragmentation space. Primary literature citations and data evaluations are catalogued with DOIs under References.

Physical framework

In contrast to global statistical treatments—such as the Los Alamos model of Madland and Nix [1], which integrates evaporation spectra over an assumed triangular residual temperature distribution—the Deterministic Sequential Emission (DSE) framework tracks individual cascade branches step by step.

Energy conservation and sequential cascade

For a given pre-neutron fragment $(A, Z)$ at initial excitation energy $E^*$, de-excitation proceeds through discrete neutron evaporation stages $k = 1, \dots, k_{\max}$. At each stage $k$, conservation of energy is evaluated explicitly:

Ē_r^(k-1) − Sₙ^(k-1) − ⟨ε⟩_k = a_k T_k²,    k = 1 … k_max

where:

  • \[\bar{E}_r^{(k-1)}\]

    is the residual excitation energy prior to the $k$-th emission, with boundary condition $\bar{E}_r^{(0)} = E^*$;

  • \[S_n^{(k-1)}\]

    is the neutron separation energy of the emitting precursor nucleus $(A - (k - 1), Z)$;

  • \[\langle\epsilon\rangle_k\]

    is the mean centre-of-mass kinetic energy of the $k$-th evaporated neutron;

  • \[a_k\]

    and $T_k$ are the nuclear level density parameter and residual nuclear temperature of the daughter nucleus $(A - k, Z)$, connected via the Fermi-gas equation of state $\bar{E}_r^{(k)} = a_k T_k^2$.

The cascade step equation is solved successively while the available excitation satisfies $\bar{E}_r^{(k-1)} \ge S_n^{(k-1)}$ and admits a physical temperature solution ($T_k > 0$), terminating at $k_{\max}$. Subsequent emissions that remain energetically possible when the emitted neutrons' kinetic energy is accounted for are evaluated against separation thresholds on $S_n$ alone. This defines the maximum allowable sequence count $n \ge k_{\max}$ for configuration $(A, Z, \text{TKE})$, yielding an average prompt neutron multiplicity:

ν(A, Z, TKE) = (n + 1) / 2

The residual excitation energy remaining after neutron cascade cessation,

E_γ = Ē_r^(n) = E* − Σ_{k=1}^n (Sₙ^(k-1) + ⟨ε⟩_k)

is retained in the residual nucleus and dissipated via prompt γ-ray cascades.

Excitation energy partition at scission

The total excitation energy (TXE) available to a complementary fragment split $(A_L, Z_L) + (A_H, Z_H)$ at total kinetic energy TKE is governed by the reaction balance:

TXE(A, Z, TKE) = Q(A, Z) + E*_CN − TKE

where $Q(A, Z)$ is the ground-state reaction Q-value and $E^*_{\text{CN}}$ is the compound nucleus excitation energy.

This energy is partitioned between light and heavy fragments using the temperature ratio $R_T(A_H) = T_L/T_H$, following the Point-by-Point (PbP) formulation of Tudora and Hambsch [2]:

E*_H / TXE = 1 / (1 + R_a R_T²(A_H))

where $R_a = a_L / a_H$ is the ratio of fragment level density parameters. In this package, $R_T(A_H)$ is obtained from the model-independent inversion of experimental prompt neutron multiplicities $\nu(A)$ implemented in FissionTemperatureRatio.jl [3].

Evaporation spectra and laboratory kinematics

Neutron evaporation from fully accelerated fragments is modeled using Weisskopf-Ewing statistical theory. Closed-form centre-of-mass evaporation spectra and their laboratory transformations follow Terrell [4], Madland and Nix [1], Märten and Seeliger [5], and Kawano et al. [6]. Multiplicity weighting and integration across fragmentations follow the evaluation standards of Capote et al. [7].

Post-neutron product distributions

Because the allowable sequence count $n$ is an integer for every configuration $(A, Z, \text{TKE})$, post-neutron product masses $A_p \in [A - n, A]$ remain discrete integers without artificial rounding.

  • Emission-probability count (default): The cascade terminates on each of its $n$ residual stages with equal probability $1/n$, yielding $P(A_p = A - k) = 1/n$ ($k = 1, \dots, n$). This formulation ensures exact mass conservation, so that the mean mass loss per fragment equals the calculated multiplicity: $\langle A - A_p \rangle = \nu$.
  • Sequence count ([products] count = "sequence_count"): Imposes $A_p = A - n$ with probability one, reproducing the convention of Tudora [8], applied to ²³⁹Pu [9] and ²³³U [10]. While representing maximum potential depletion, it removes $\langle n \rangle \approx 2\nu - 1$ mass units on average.
  • Post-neutron product kinetic energies $E_{K,p} = E_K (A_p / A)$ and total product kinetic energy $\text{TKE}_p = E_{K,p,L} + E_{K,p,H}$ follow the kinematic relations of Tudora [8], assuming isotropic emission leaves average fragment velocities unchanged.

Provenance and literature lineage

The DSE framework was introduced by Tudora, Hambsch, and Tobosaru [11], with systematic residual properties established in [12], experimental validations in [13], and comparative analysis with Point-by-Point modeling in [14]. Nuclear masses, reaction Q-values, and separation energies are taken from the AME2020 evaluation [15], [16]. Isobaric charge distributions follow Wahl's $Z_p$ model [17], [18]. Nuclear level densities are calculated using the Back-Shifted Fermi Gas model of von Egidy and Bucurescu [19], [20] and the composite formula of Gilbert and Cameron [21].

This package is a modern, modular Julia re-implementation of the computational code originally developed for the author's 2023 MSc thesis (Faculty of Physics, University of Bucharest, supervised by Prof. Anabella Tudora). Where current defaults refine earlier formulations (such as mass-conserving emission-probability counts and per-neutron spectral weighting), options are provided to reproduce the original thesis results identically.

DeterministicSequentialEmission.DeterministicSequentialEmission — Module
DeterministicSequentialEmission

Prompt neutron emission from fission fragments, treated sequence by sequence.

The model solves the energy conservation equation of every emission sequence separately,

Ē_r^(k-1) − Sₙ^(k-1) − ⟨ε⟩_k = a_k T_k²,    k = 1 … n(A,Z,TKE)

with Weisskopf-Ewing evaporation spectra in the centre-of-mass system and fragment level densities in the Fermi-gas regime. Because the number of sequences n is an integer at every configuration, every product mass A − 1 … A − n is an integer, and the post-neutron products are distributed without rounding: by default a chain ends on each of its n residuals with probability 1/n, so that the products lose the multiplicity, and the published count, A_p = A − n, is an option.
