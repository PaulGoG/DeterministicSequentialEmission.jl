DeterministicSequentialEmission

Prompt neutron emission from fission fragments, treated sequence by sequence. The literature behind the formulation and the input data is listed with DOIs under References.

The model solves the energy conservation equation of every emission sequence separately,

Ē_r^(k-1) − Sₙ^(k-1) − ⟨ε⟩_k = a_k T_k²,    k = 1 … k_max

with Weisskopf-Ewing evaporation spectra in the centre-of-mass system and fragment level densities in the Fermi-gas regime. Ē_r^(k) = a_k T_k² is the residual excitation energy after the k-th emission, with Ē_r^(0) = E*, the excitation energy of the fragment; Sₙ^(k-1) is the neutron separation energy of the nucleus that emits the k-th neutron; ⟨ε⟩_k is the mean centre-of-mass energy of that neutron; a_k and T_k are the level density parameter and the temperature of the k-th residual nucleus. The equation is solved for k = 1 … k_max, while the residual nucleus can afford Sₙ and the equation admits a temperature. The emissions that remain possible once the neutrons' kinetic energy is returned to the residual are then counted on Sₙ alone, which gives the number of sequences n ≥ k_max of the configuration (A, Z, TKE) and its multiplicity ν = (n+1)/2.

Because n is an integer at every configuration, every product mass A − 1 … A − n is an integer, so the products are distributed without rounding. By default a chain ends on each of its n residuals with probability 1/n, so the mass its products lose is its multiplicity; [products] count = "sequence_count" takes the published A_p = A − n [1] instead.

The physics is the literature's; the implementation is mine. The model is that of Tudora, Hambsch and Tobosaru [2], with the systematics of its residual quantities [3], its validation against measurement [4], its post-neutron fragment yields [1] and its place beside the point-by-point model [5]. The partition of the total excitation energy by the temperature ratio and the evaporation framework are those of the point-by-point model [6]; the extraction of the temperature ratio from measured multiplicities, which FissionTemperatureRatio.jl implements, is [7]. The evaporation spectrum in closed form and its transformation to the laboratory follow Madland and Nix [8] and Terrell [9]; the conventions of the evaluated spectra are those of Capote et al. [10]. This package is my Julia implementation of that physics.

A run is driven by a TOML configuration and writes its tables; see Running a case, which also documents the configuration and the pipeline. The vocabulary every name in this package is drawn from is set out in Naming. What the model takes from the fragments before emission begins — nuclides and systems, masses, the charge distribution and the fragmentation domain, level densities, the energy balance, yields and the temperature-ratio run record — lives in the domain package, summarised under The fragmentation domain. The reference of this package follows the order a configuration is consumed in: the excitation-energy partition, then the evaporation, the emission chain and the spectrum.
