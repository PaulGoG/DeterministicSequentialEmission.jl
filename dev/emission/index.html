Emission and spectrum

The evaporation cross section, the sequence-by-sequence solution it drives, and the prompt neutron spectrum that follows from it. The sequential solution and the sequence average are the model of Tudora, Hambsch and Tobosaru [2], with the systematics of its residual quantities [3]; the evaporation spectrum in closed form is Madland and Nix [8], its laboratory transformation and the probability of emission along a chain follow Terrell [9], Märten and Seeliger [11] and Kawano et al. [12], and the weights of the total spectrum are the conventions of the evaluated spectra [10].

DeterministicSequentialEmission.MINIMUM_TEMPERATURE — Constant
MINIMUM_TEMPERATURE

Lower bound on an acceptable residual temperature, in MeV; the convention of the thesis.

A numerical floor, not the crossover from the Fermi-gas to the constant-temperature regime, which lies near T ≈ 0.5–0.8 MeV for fission fragments. It rejects roots only where Ē_r − Sₙ has fallen below about 17 keV, truncating a chain that is already ending. Applied unconditionally, unlike the optional upper bound.

DeterministicSequentialEmission.ConstantCrossSection — Type
ConstantCrossSection()

Energy-independent σ_c(ε) = σ₀.

The evaporation spectrum and its first moment become

φ(ε; T) = (ε/T²) exp(−ε/T)
⟨ε⟩(T)  = 2T

so the sequential equation a T² + 2T − (Ē_r − Sₙ) = 0 is quadratic with the positive root

T = [√(1 + a(Ē_r − Sₙ)) − 1] / a

DeterministicSequentialEmission.EvaporationCrossSection — Type
EvaporationCrossSection

Analytical form of the cross section for compound nucleus formation in the process inverse to evaporation, σ_c(ε).

The choice determines how the sequential equations are solved: a constant cross section reduces them to a quadratic with a closed-form root, while an energy-dependent one makes them transcendental.

DeterministicSequentialEmission.VariableCrossSection — Type
VariableCrossSection()

Iwamoto's energy-dependent form, a constant term plus one carrying the s-wave neutron strength function,

σ_c(ε) = σ₀ (1 + α/√ε),   α = π ħ² S₀ / (m r₀² A^(2/3))

which gives

φ(ε; T) = (ε + α√ε) exp(−ε/T) / [T^(3/2)(√T + α√π/2)]
⟨ε⟩(T)  = T (2√T + 3α√π/4) / (√T + α√π/2)

The sequential equation is then transcendental and is solved numerically. It has exactly one positive root: f(0) = −(Ē_r − Sₙ) < 0, f → +∞, and f′ > 0 throughout, with f′(0) = 3/2.

DeterministicSequentialEmission.neutron_strength_function — Method
neutron_strength_function(A) -> Float64

The s-wave neutron strength function S₀(A): a piecewise-linear interpolation, with plateaux, through selected nuclei of the RIPL-3 s-wave resonance table, Capote et al., Nucl. Data Sheets 110, 3107 (2009), doi:10.1016/j.nds.2009.10.004 — ²³Na, ⁵⁵Mn, ¹²⁰Sn, ¹⁴⁶Nd, ¹⁵²Gd among the nodes — not a least-squares fit. Returned bare, not in the customary units of 10⁻⁴: S₀(140) is 2.33e-4, as alpha_parameter expects. In cross-validation against the table it describes the stable nuclei as well as any function of A alone; as such a function it cannot carry the Z-dependence of S₀, largest at A = 111–124 and across the N = 82 closure at A = 136–146, and nothing is measured for the neutron-rich fragments 4–8 neutrons beyond the table. The resulting α runs from 0.037 at A = 120 to 1.24 at A = 146. The closed-form ⟨ε⟩(T) of the variable cross section was compared with an optical-model (Becchetti–Greenlees) calculation by Tudora, Hambsch and Tobosaru, Eur. Phys. J. A 54, 87 (2018), doi:10.1140/epja/i2018-12521-7, §2.1, over A = 76–160 and S₀ = 0.7–1.1 × 10⁻⁴, i.e. α = 0.21–0.55, agreeing within 4 % for T = 0.2–2 MeV; about half of the fragment masses fall outside that envelope here. The observables are weakly sensitive to the choice: across the alternatives tried, ν(A) is unchanged, ⟨ε⟩(A) moves by at most 0.13 MeV at one mass, ⟨E⟩ by under 1.5 % and the ²⁵²Cf spectrum ratio at 10 MeV by under 2 %.

DeterministicSequentialEmission.EmissionChain — Type
EmissionChain

The full sequential emission from one initial fragment at one (A, Z, TKE), characterised by two distinct counts.

Fields

  • possible: n, the number of energetically possible emissions. The chain continues while the residual can afford a separation energy once the kinetic energy carried away by the neutrons already emitted is returned. n is an integer, so every product mass A − 1 … A − n is an integer without rounding.   • solved: k_max ≤ n, the number of sequences whose equation admits an acceptable temperature. T, Ē_r and ⟨ε⟩ are averaged over these k_max sequences only.

DeterministicSequentialEmission.SequenceWeighting — Type
SequenceWeighting

How the emission sequences of one fragment are weighted in a quantity carried by its neutrons: the centre-of-mass and laboratory spectra and the mean neutron energy.

neutron_multiplicity is ν = (n+1)/2, the mean of a number of emitted neutrons that is equiprobable on 1 … n. The k-th neutron of the chain is then emitted with probability P(N ≥ k) = (n+1−k)/n, and a quantity averaged over the emitted neutrons carries that weight:

⟨q⟩ = Σ_k (n+1−k) q_k / Σ_k (n+1−k)

EmissionProbabilityWeighting, the default, applies it. The weights fall linearly along the chain, as the probability of reaching a step does in every treatment that carries a distribution of excitation energy whose width is comparable with one neutron step: Terrell, Phys. Rev. 113, 527 (1959), doi:10.1103/PhysRev.113.527; Märten and Seeliger, J. Phys. G 10, 349 (1984), doi:10.1088/0305-4616/10/3/010; Kawano et al., Phys. Rev. C 104, 014611 (2021), doi:10.1103/PhysRevC.104.014611. UniformSequenceWeighting counts every sequence once, ⟨q⟩ = (1/k_max) Σ_k q_k, the operator of eq. (12) of Tudora, EPJA 54, 87 (2018), doi:10.1140/epja/i2018-12521-7, and of the thesis. It takes the last neutron of a chain as emitted as often as the first, which over-represents the cold end of the chain and gives a spectrum softer than the multiplicity it goes with implies.

The weighting applies to what the neutrons carry. The residual temperature and the residual energy written against fragment mass belong to the nucleus the chain ends on; with the count equiprobable on 1 … n each residual is the last with the same probability, so their average over the sequences is uniform under either weighting.

DeterministicSequentialEmission.emission_chain — Method
emission_chain(table, model, cross_section, fragment, excitation; kwargs...)
    -> EmissionChain

Solve the sequential equations for one fragment, starting from its excitation energy at full acceleration. Two stages separate k_max from n.

The first solves

Ē_r^(k-1) − Sₙ^(k-1) − ⟨ε⟩_k = a_k T_k²

while the residual can afford the separation energy, consuming Sₙ + ⟨ε⟩ per step. It stops at the first sequence whose equation admits no acceptable temperature.

The second returns the kinetic energy carried away by the neutrons emitted so far and continues on Sₙ alone, counting the emissions that remain energetically possible. Those sequences carry no temperature but do count towards n.

DeterministicSequentialEmission.gamma_energy — Method
gamma_energy(chain, excitation) -> Float64

The excitation energy of a fragment that its neutrons leave, in MeV: what the model has left for prompt γ rays, conversion electrons and isomers. Under the count of neutron_multiplicity, the chain ending equiprobably after 1 … n emissions, the k-th neutron is emitted with probability P(N ≥ k) = (n+1−k)/n and carries Sₙ^(k) + ⟨ε⟩_k away:

E_γ = E* − Σ_k P(N ≥ k) (Sₙ^(k) + ⟨ε⟩_k)

A sequence of the second stage carries no temperature and is charged its separation energy alone, ⟨ε⟩_k = 0, as the spectrum charges it, so the balance `E* = Σk P(N ≥ k)(Sₙ^(k) + ⟨ε⟩k)

  • Eγcloses on the energies the run's neutrons are given. The endings beyondkmax`, taken

alone, would close below zero, since the second stage counts on Sₙ once the kinetic energy has been returned; the mean over the endings is what the count predicts. The weights are those of EmissionProbabilityWeighting whatever the spectrum's weighting, since they belong to the count. A chain that emits nothing leaves its whole excitation.

DeterministicSequentialEmission.neutron_multiplicity — Method
neutron_multiplicity(chain) -> Float64

The prompt neutron multiplicity ν, zero for a chain that emits nothing.

ν is the sequence average of the emission index. Every per-sequence quantity q_k is reduced by the same operator, eq. (12) of Tudora, EPJA 54, 87 (2018), doi:10.1140/epja/i2018-12521-7; eq. (4) of Tudora, EPJA 56, 84 (2020), doi:10.1140/epja/s10050-020-00059-2; eq. (20) of Tudora, EPJ Web Conf. 256, 00017 (2021), doi:10.1051/epjconf/202125600017,

⟨q⟩ = (1/n) Σ_{k=1}^{n} q_k

evaluated at q_k = k, so ν = (1 + 2 + … + n)/n = (n+1)/2. It is the multiplicity of the averaged emission sequence and the quantity compared with measurement. It differs from n, the number of sequences, which post_neutron_mass uses.

DeterministicSequentialEmission.post_neutron_mass — Method
post_neutron_mass(chain) -> Int

The mass number of the last residual fragment, A_p = A − n, an integer by construction.

A_p uses n, not ν: it is a property of the chain, the nucleus on which the cascade ends, rather than a sequence average. A global treatment yields only an averaged ν(A,Z,TKE), whose A − ν must be rounded (Tudora, EPJA 58, 126 (2022), doi:10.1140/epja/s10050-022-00766-y, §2.4). Consequently ⟨A⟩ − ⟨A_p⟩ equals the yield average of n, not ⟨ν⟩. It is the product mass under SequenceCount; the default EmissionProbabilityCount of product_masses spreads the product over the chain's n residuals, and the mass loss is then ν.

DeterministicSequentialEmission.SpectrumAverage — Type
SpectrumAverage

How the pair spectra of the fragmentations combine into the total laboratory spectrum.

PerNeutronAverage, the default, weights each unit-normalised pair spectrum by the yield times the pair multiplicity, N = Σ Y ν_pair N_pair / Σ Y ν_pair. Every emitted neutron counts once, so this is the unit-area shape χ/ν̄ of the neutron yield per fission that evaluated spectra represent: eq. (99) of Capote et al., Nucl. Data Sheets 131, 1 (2016), doi:10.1016/j.nds.2015.12.002. It is also the weight of eq. (53) of Madland and Nix, Nucl. Sci. Eng. 81, 213 (1982), doi:10.13182/NSE82-5, across fission chances, and of eqs. (8)–(9) of Kawano et al., Phys. Rev. C 104, 014611 (2021), doi:10.1103/PhysRevC.104.014611, across fragment pairs. PerFissionAverage weights it by the yield alone, N = Σ Y N_pair / Σ Y, the form of the thesis and of Tudora, Hambsch, Tobosaru, EPJA 54, 87 (2018), doi:10.1140/epja/i2018-12521-7, text after eq. (16). The two differ where ν_pair correlates with the hardness of N_pair, and the per-fission form under-weights the hot, high-multiplicity fragmentations.

DeterministicSequentialEmission.centre_of_mass_spectrum — Method
centre_of_mass_spectrum(cross_section, α, T, ε) -> Float64

The Weisskopf-Ewing evaporation spectrum of one emission sequence, in the centre-of-mass frame of the emitting fragment, normalized to unit area.

φ(ε; T) = K(T) ε σ_c(ε) exp(−ε/T),   K(T) = [∫₀^∞ ε σ_c(ε) exp(−ε/T) dε]⁻¹

σ₀ cancels in K, so only the shape of σ_c survives. With a constant cross section this is ε exp(−ε/T)/T²; with Iwamoto's σ₀(1 + α/√ε) it is (ε + α√ε) exp(−ε/T) / [T^(3/2)(√T + α√π/2)]. Both integrate to 1, and their first moments are 2T and mean_neutron_energy respectively.

α is ignored by the constant cross section and is the residual nucleus's alpha_parameter for the variable one, the value the sequential equation was solved with, so the spectrum is consistent with the energy balance that produced T.

chain_spectrum(chain, cross_section, E_f, E[, weighting]) -> Float64

The laboratory spectrum of one fragment, averaged over the emission sequences that carry a temperature.

DeterministicSequentialEmission.laboratory_spectrum — Method
laboratory_spectrum(cross_section, α, T, E_f, E) -> Float64

One emission sequence's spectrum transformed into the laboratory frame.

A neutron leaving a fragment that moves with kinetic energy per nucleon E_f arrives with E = ε + E_f + 2√(ε E_f) cos θ. Isotropy in the centre-of-mass makes cos θ uniform, so at fixed ε the lab energy is uniform over a band of width 4√(ε E_f), and folding over φ:

N(E) = 1/(4√E_f) ∫_{u₁}^{u₂} φ(ε) ε^(-1/2) dε,   u_{1,2} = (√E ∓ √E_f)²

The factor 4√(ε E_f) is the width of that band, by the identity (√ε+√E_f)² − (√ε−√E_f)² = 4√(ε E_f), so the kernel is measure-preserving and N inherits unit area from φ exactly. With ε = s² the kernel is uniform in s over an interval of fixed length 2√E_f,

N(E) = 1/(2√E_f) ∫_{|√E−√E_f|}^{√E+√E_f} φ(s²) ds

Evaluated in closed form rather than by quadrature. With a_i = u_i/T,

constant σ_c:   N = [Γ(3/2,a₁) − Γ(3/2,a₂)] / (4√(E_f T))
variable σ_c:   N = { [Γ(3/2,a₁) − Γ(3/2,a₂)] + (α/√T)[exp(−a₁) − exp(−a₂)] }
                    / [4√E_f (√T + α√π/2)]

The second reduces to the first as α → 0. The differences are taken over the upper incomplete gamma, since the equivalent lower-gamma difference cancels catastrophically in the tail and is in error by a factor of four beyond about 20 MeV.

Source: eq. (12) of Madland & Nix, Nucl. Sci. Eng. 81, 213 (1982), doi:10.13182/NSE82-5.

Post-emission distributions

A fragment's emission chain ends on a product of the same charge, before β decay, and of mass A_p ≤ A. The number of possible sequences n is an integer at every configuration (A, Z, TKE), so every product mass is an integer and the products are distributed without rounding. What remains to be chosen is how many of the n possible neutrons a product has lost; ProductCount carries that choice, and [products] count selects it.

  • emission_probability, the default, EmissionProbabilityCount. The chain ends on each of its n residuals with probability 1/n,

    P(A_p = A − k) = 1/n,    k = 1 … n,

    and a fragment that emits nothing stays at A_p = A. This is the reading the multiplicity and the spectrum already rest on: ν = (n+1)/2 is the mean of a count equiprobable on 1 … n, and the k-th neutron is emitted with probability (n+1−k)/n. Applied to the product, it makes the mass the products lose equal to the multiplicity, ⟨A − A_p⟩ = ν, configuration by configuration. It is the uniform average over the n endings of a chain that eq. (12) of [1] takes for the mean product kinetic energy, applied to the mass as well.

  • sequence_count, SequenceCount. A_p = A − n with probability one: the prescription of [1], of its applications to ²³⁹Pu(nth,f) [13] and ²³³U(nth,f) [14], and of the code submitted with my thesis.

The default departs from the published count, and does so deliberately. Subtracting all n removes ⟨n⟩ ≈ 2ν − 1 neutrons per fragment on average: 2.50 against ν = 1.74 in the two ²⁵²Cf(sf) runs of my thesis, so that the product yields of one run imply 44 % more neutrons per fission than its own multiplicity. Without fine-grained excitation-energy input the sequence average stands in for the distribution, and a chain that can emit n neutrons emits (n+1)/2 of them on average; a product distribution that always removes n is that of the hottest ending alone. The published count stays one key away, and with it the test suite reproduces the post-neutron mass yields of both thesis runs to within 10⁻⁸ relative.

The yields follow from the fragment yield Y(A, Z, TKE) and the product probabilities P,

Y_p(Z, A_p) = 𝒩 Σ_{A, TKE} Y(A, Z, TKE) P(A_p | A, Z, TKE),
Y_p(A_p) = Σ_Z Y_p(Z, A_p),    Y_p(N_p) = Σ_{A_p − Z = N_p} Y_p(Z, A_p),

the sums running over the configurations solved. 𝒩 makes Σ Y_p(A_p) equal to the total of the input yield, [yield] total, 200 for both fragments of every fission: the solved configurations stand for the whole distribution, as they do in the average multiplicity. Emission leaves the charge alone, so Y_p(Z) is the Y(Z) of the solved configurations and is not written. At A₀/2 a configuration is the heavy fragment of one labelling of a split and the light fragment of the mirror labelling, and the self-complementary nuclide (A₀/2, Z₀/2) is both fragments of one; each configuration enters once, through the chain its multiplicity is taken from, as the input yield counts it once.

The kinetic energies are those of eqs. (6)–(9) of [1],

E_K = TKE (A₀ − A)/A₀,    E_K_p = E_K A_p/A,    TKE_p = E_K_p,L + E_K_p,H,

emission isotropic in the fragment frame leaving the fragment velocity unchanged on average. E_K_p(A_p) and E_K_p(Z) are means over Y P. TKE_p is taken over the two products of a pair, independent of each other, with the joint probability P_L P_H and the pair weight that halves a mass-symmetric split, as in the spectrum; it is written against the heavy product mass. Under the sequence count these are eqs. (8) and (9) of the paper; under the default the mean of E_K_p over the endings of a chain is its eq. (12).

A run writes Y_vs_A_p.dat, the independent yields Y_vs_Z_A_p.dat, Y_vs_N_p.dat, E_K_p_vs_A_p.dat, E_K_p_vs_Z.dat and TKE_p_vs_A_p.dat, and adds mean_product_mass_loss, ⟨A − A_p⟩ per fragment, and mean_TKE_p to totals.dat.

DeterministicSequentialEmission.ProductCount — Type
ProductCount

How many neutrons a fragment's emission chain removes from its mass: the rule that turns a chain of n possible sequences into the distribution of the post-neutron product mass A_p. The charge is unchanged, since the products are taken before β decay.

EmissionProbabilityCount, the default, ends the chain on each of its n residuals with probability 1/n, the reading that gives neutron_multiplicity ν = (n+1)/2 and the emission probabilities (n+1−k)/n of EmissionProbabilityWeighting. The mass the products lose is then the multiplicity, ⟨A − A_p⟩ = ν, configuration by configuration. SequenceCount subtracts all n, the prescription of Tudora, EPJA 58, 126 (2022), doi:10.1140/epja/s10050-022-00766-y, under which the products lose ⟨n⟩ ≈ 2ν − 1.

DeterministicSequentialEmission._product_distributions — Method
_product_distributions(chains, weights, compound_mass, count, total) -> NamedTuple

Contract the emission chains of a run into the post-neutron product distributions under the ProductCount count. chains is the list run_pipeline builds, one (fragmentation, TKE, light, heavy, split) per solved configuration; weights the yield weights keyed by (A, Z, TKE); compound_mass A₀.

Each fragment configuration (A, Z, TKE) with a chain and a weight w contributes once, through the chain its multiplicity is taken from (a later occurrence replaces an earlier one, as in the multiplicity store), with w p at each product mass (A_p, p) of product_masses:

Y_p(A_p) = 𝒩 Σ w p,    Y_p(Z, A_p) = 𝒩 Σ_{Z fixed} w p,    Y_p(N_p = A_p − Z) = 𝒩 Σ w p

At A₀/2 a configuration is reached from both labellings of a split, and twice from the self-complementary one; it is counted once, as the input yield counts it. 𝒩 = total / Σ w, the sum running over the configurations solved, so that Σ_{A_p} Y_p(A_p) = total: the products of the solved configurations stand for the whole distribution, as in the multiplicity contraction.

The kinetic energies are E_K = TKE (A₀ − A)/A₀ and E_K_p = E_K A_p/A, the velocity unchanged on average; E_K_p(A_p) and E_K_p(Z) are means over w p. TKE_p = E_K_p,L + E_K_p,H is taken over the pairs, the two products independent, with weight w_pair p_L p_H, w_pair the heavy fragment's weight halved at mass symmetry (_pair_weight), and keyed by the heavy product mass. Means with no positive weight are omitted.

Returns mass_yield, yield ((Z, A_p, Y), sorted), neutron_yield, kinetic_energy, kinetic_energy_by_charge, total_kinetic_energy, and the totals mass_loss, the mean ⟨A − A_p⟩ per fragment over w, and mean_total_kinetic_energy, the mean TKE_p over the pairs.

DeterministicSequentialEmission.product_masses — Method
product_masses(chain, count) -> Vector{Tuple{Int, Float64}}

The post-neutron product masses of the fragment chain starts from, with their probabilities, under the ProductCount count. The probabilities sum to one. n is chain.possible, the number of possible sequences, those without a temperature included.

  • EmissionProbabilityCount: (A − k, 1/n) for k = 1 … n; (A, 1) for n = 0.
  • SequenceCount: (A − n, 1).

Examples

julia> chain = EmissionChain(Nuclide(50, 132), [
           EmissionSequence(1, 7.0, 13.0, 0.9, 10.5, 1.4),
           EmissionSequence(2, 5.5, 13.1, nothing, nothing, nothing),
       ]);

julia> product_masses(chain, EmissionProbabilityCount())
2-element Vector{Tuple{Int64, Float64}}:
 (131, 0.5)
 (130, 0.5)

julia> product_masses(chain, SequenceCount())
1-element Vector{Tuple{Int64, Float64}}:
 (130, 1.0)
